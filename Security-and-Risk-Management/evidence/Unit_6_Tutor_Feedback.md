# Unit 6 Development Team Project – Tutor Feedback

**Assessment:** Risk Identification Report  
**Overall grade:** 77% (provisional)

## Knowledge and Understanding – 19/25

The tutor found strong knowledge of operational, cybersecurity, regulatory and supply-chain risks. The use of ISO 31000, STRIDE and qualitative scenarios was well justified, particularly the recognition that STRIDE could not assess food safety, staffing capacity or supplier economics. The likelihood-impact scale was not fully defined, however, and a formal register linking assets, threats, vulnerabilities, consequences and risk levels would have improved transparency.

## Application of Knowledge and Understanding – 20/25

The controls and phased proposal were relevant to the organisation and preserved its local identity and supply resilience. The tutor recommended identifying control owners, implementation timescales and residual risks, and examining the practical cost and staffing implications of cloud services, security controls and supplier monitoring.

## Criticality – 21/25

The strongest feature was the challenge to the 50%, 24% and 33% claims and the analysis of their interaction. The reversible pilot was also viewed positively. A clearer side-by-side comparison of the status quo and digitalisation, together with more consideration of affordability and acceptable residual risk, would have strengthened the decision.

## Presentation and Structure – 17/25

The report followed a logical structure and used relevant academic sources. The tutor found it visually dense, with small text and limited spacing. Properly formatted tables, a new page for the references and correction of a minor punctuation error were recommended.

## Actions Carried Forward

- Define scales before scoring.
- Use a risk register that shows the reasoning chain.
- Assign owners and timescales and state residual risk.
- Make option comparison and risk acceptance explicit.
- Treat readability as part of analytical quality.
